## 2. Correctness wording

| Wording | Decision |
|---|---|
| "92% of the 553 values HiveBlot extracted were correct" + "blind test" in the same sentence | **GO** |
| "92% of 553 values HiveBlot stated matched a human annotator" | GO (v1 wording) |
| "92% accurate", "92% accuracy", "accurate 92% of the time" | NO-GO. Accuracy in the report means coverage (57%). |
| "93%" | NO-GO (round down) |
| "92%" with no denominator or no "blind test" | NO-GO |

"Correct" is acceptable for a lay reader because each of the 553 values was checked against a label made

## 4. Requested claims, go / no-go

| Claim | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Only tool on the internet that does this" | **NO-GO** | Unverifiable universal negative; no search was done. A narrower form ("the only open tool we know of that turns Western-blot figures into lane-level, source-linked records") becomes usable only after a dated, logged search of PubMed/Google Scholar/bioRxiv (figure mining, blot extraction), GitHub, and commercial lab-data/ELN and literature-mining products, with each near-competitor and why it differs written down. Even then say "we know of", never "only". |
| "Saves biologists X% of time" | **NO-GO** | No biologist's workflow was timed; one annotator, 3 papers; review and gap-filling were not timed. Under the assumption model in SPEED.md (57% coverage, review 25-50% of manual time) the saving is 2-27%, not 84-95%. |
| "Took 84% less time than manual labeling on the same papers, before review" | GO in fuller text only | Measured whole-paper figure; the "before review" qualifier is mandatory. |
| Panel recall 91%, tier (iii) 98%, per-field claims | NO-GO | Unchanged from v1. |

## 6. Fuller version (2 sentences)

In a pre-registered blind test on 3 papers, 92% of the 553 Western-blot values HiveBlot extracted were
correct against labels a teammate made from the papers alone, and it filled in 57% of the values she
labeled. Its full run took about 20 minutes versus about 2 hours of hand labeling on the same papers
(about 6x faster), not counting the time a researcher still spends reviewing its output.

## 7. Interview answers

**"How was correctness measured?"** We pre-registered the method, sealed HiveBlot's output on 3 randomly
drawn papers, had a teammate with Western-blot experience label the blots from the PDFs without seeing it,
then scored every value HiveBlot stated against her labels, allowing only notation fixes she confirmed.

**"How did you measure speed?"** During the same test, my annotator logged how long she spent labeling, about
two hours for 8 panels across 3 papers, and the pipeline logged its own wall-clock time for each paper,
about 20 minutes in total. I compared HiveBlot's whole run against her time on only the panels she chose,
which is the conservative comparison and gives about 6x; per panel it is closer to 20x, but that is an
estimate because we did not timestamp each image. Neither number includes reviewing HiveBlot's output, and
with 57% coverage a researcher still has to check it and fill gaps, so I don't claim a time-saved percentage.

## 8. Must NOT say

- "93%", "accuracy", "accurate 92% of the time", "92% accurate".
- "against a biologist", "expert-validated", "independent".
- "553 facts extracted" or "all facts"; "98%"; "91% recall".
- "Only tool on the internet", "first", "only", "unique" (no logged search).
- "Saves biologists X%", "X% time saved", or any speed figure that omits "before review" when a percent is used.
- "20x faster" as a measured number.
- Anything about treatment, protein-identity, or cell-line accuracy; "validated"; "generalizes".
